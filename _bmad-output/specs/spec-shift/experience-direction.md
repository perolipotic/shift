# Experience Direction

Who each role is and what they come here to do. This is the standing motivation every surface is judged against; it is not an interface specification. The realized interface contract is `EXPERIENCE.md` (information architecture, behaviour, states, flows) and `DESIGN.md` (visual system) — both adopted companions, both binding, neither restated here. Vocabulary per `glossary.md`.

## Member

Three standing questions, answered without navigation, on a phone, in seconds: *when do I next work*, *how many hours have I done*, *how much leave is left*.

A member opens the app between other tasks — in a vehicle, mid-shift, before bed. Anything requiring a second tap to answer the first question is a failure of the product, not of the member.

## Admin

Configure once, then be told when something needs a decision. Admins currently own the spreadsheet and are the organization's single point of failure for the rota; the product's job is to remove that dependency without making them feel demoted from it.

**They are non-technical.** Every configuration surface must be usable without knowing what an offset or an anchor date is called. This binds hardest on rotation configuration, which is the product's most conceptually loaded screen and the one where the vocabulary of the engine is most tempting to expose.
